<ProcessStep id="desk-research" title="Desk research" first>
	<p>Grounded the redesign in proven ecommerce and UX patterns for category and product pages, rather than guessing at conventions for a marketplace format that was new to ING.</p>
</ProcessStep>

<ProcessStep id="expert-review" title="Expert review">
	<p>Ran a quick pass to locate the highest-pain areas first, so limited time went to the issues most likely to block scaling — not a full page-by-page audit.</p>
</ProcessStep>

<ProcessStep id="benchmark" title="Benchmark">
	<p>Chose 10 competitors/ecommerce parties together with the Squad, so findings were rooted in patterns users would already recognize, not personal design preference.</p>
</ProcessStep>

<ProcessStep id="prioritize" title="Prioritize by impact, not by category">
	<p>Findings from the previous three steps were rated on severity/impact and grouped into slices, so the Product Owner could greenlight scope by user value instead of page-by-page guesswork.</p>
	<ImageBlock src={severityImpactList} alt="Usability findings rated on severity, impact and priority with proposed solutions" variant="hatch" />
</ProcessStep>

<ProcessStep id="first-designs" title="First designs">
	<p>Slices with big to medium impact were approved by the Product Owner. I designed the bookkeeping category first, deliberately choosing the most-used category so the new scalable pattern would be proven under the heaviest real-world load before being rolled out anywhere else.</p>
</ProcessStep>

<ProcessStep id="persona-walkthroughs" title="Persona walkthroughs & squad review">
	<p>Walked through the designs as each persona to surface gaps before a single line of code was written, then presented the design back to the squad as a story from each persona's perspective, so the reasoning landed, not just the screens.</p>
	<p>The assumption personas were shaped using patterns from the actual design, to make them easy to follow and check when going through the designs. The pill elements were actual filters the user could set, and the important decision criteria were used to fill and evaluate the product cards.</p>
	<ImageBlock src={personas} alt="Three assumption personas: starter, ZZP and MKB, with decision criteria" />
</ProcessStep>

<ProcessStep id="full-design" title="Full design & scalable components" last>
	<p>After processing feedback from users and the squad, I finalized the design for all screen sizes and built every component to be reusable across all current and upcoming categories, instead of designing each category from scratch.</p>
</ProcessStep>

	</div>
</ProcessSection>

<EndProductSection
	heading="The End Product"
	intro="Two pages carry the marketplace: the category page for comparing, the product detail page for deciding. Both were rebuilt as configurable components so any future category could reuse them."
>
	<Decision id="product-1" badge="01" eyebrow="Compare before diving in" title="Category Page" first>
		<p>Page that shows all products from a certain product category, with the purpose of easy comparison before diving into the details on the product detail page.</p>
		<SubFeatureGrid>
			<div>
				<h4>Filters</h4>
				<p>A smarter filter area with the possibility to choose multiple filters, see the ones selected, and remove them easily. It's also more scalable, because the pattern can be repeated endlessly — just add another (single- or multi-select) dropdown.</p>
			</div>
			<div>
				<h4>Sorting</h4>
				<p>Optimized the space by placing the label next to it and de-emphasizing it by losing the underline, because the focus should be on the products. Also changed the sorting from alphabetical to price — users came to this platform with no idea of the providers or their product names.</p>
			</div>
			<div>
				<h4>Product cards</h4>
				<p>Cleaned up the product card and created two sections for easier scanning: the header and the product details. Removed the USPs, because they weren't comparable to each other, and replaced them with three overlapping main attributes (category-dependent). Created designs for a fully configurable component.</p>
			</div>
		</SubFeatureGrid>
		<BeforeAfter
			before={{ src: oldCategoryPage, alt: 'Old category page with alphabetical, single-select filters' }}
			after={{ src: newCategoryPage, alt: 'New category page with removable multi-select filter chips, sorted by price low to high' }}
		/>
	</Decision>

	<Decision id="product-2" badge="02" eyebrow="Inform before deciding" title="Product Detail Page">
		<p>Page that shows all details of a certain product, with the purpose of informing the user so they can make an informed decision before continuing to the partner website to buy the product or service.</p>
		<SubFeatureGrid>
			<div>
				<h4>From partners to configurable products</h4>
				<p>The squad had taken the wrong path. The name chosen for the project, ING + Partners, had its information architecture based on partners. This meant clicking a product and then seeing a partner page with a subset of products. By changing this into one product per detail page, the page became consistent with user expectations again.</p>
			</div>
			<div>
				<h4>Focusing on the fold</h4>
				<p>I wanted the user to have a quick overview of the product and the available information first, before diving into more in-depth information. I reached that by advocating focus on primary, summarizing content and quick navigation above the fold.</p>
			</div>
			<div>
				<h4>Sticky product summary card</h4>
				<p>Whenever users were convinced by the extra information on the detail page, they shouldn't need to start looking for the primary CTA. Next to this, the area for the detail page content was too wide for easy reading. By introducing a product summary card that always stays (sticks) in the upper right corner, both were addressed.</p>
			</div>
		</SubFeatureGrid>
		<BeforeAfter
			src={beforeAfterDetailPage}
			alt="Detail page before and after: partner-branded page with buried pricing next to single-product page with summary card upper right"
			labels={['Before', 'After']}
		/>
		<ImageBlock src={genericCardComponent} alt="Generic product card spec with extremes and rendered examples" />
	</Decision>

	<ExtendingImpact eyebrow="Beyond the two pages" title="Extending the Impact">
		<ExtendingItem title="User testing" first>
			<p>Set up and conducted user tests with the most complex product category: payment solutions. Received an Average System Usability Score of 75 (68 is above average), with clear insights on what could be improved.</p>
		</ExtendingItem>
		<ExtendingItem title="Designing compare & select">
			<p>Let users select a sub-set of products within a category and view them side-by-side in a comparison table, instead of comparing full product pages across separate tabs. Users were already asking for it, and competitor analysis showed mature ecommerce platforms offered the same pattern for products in the consideration stage of the funnel. Desk research also showed a <strong>5% to 15%</strong> lift in conversion rates for moderate-consideration goods, which made the business case straightforward.</p>
			<ImageBlock src={compareSelect} alt="Compare and select flow: card checkboxes, compare page and parameter table" />
		</ExtendingItem>
