<KeyFindings heading="Key findings from the 0-measurement" intro="Five findings set the brief for the redesign — three from users, two from the business and the platform we were framed into.">
	<div>
		<h4>From users</h4>
		<ul>
			<li>Users struggled with De Hypotheker's input set — several fields weren't needed for the calculation, while a field to enter their own money or surplus house value was missing entirely.</li>
			<li>Users wanted to see "kosten koper" (additional buyer's costs like notary fees) to judge whether their savings would actually cover the purchase.</li>
			<li>Mobile users on Funda arrived after a large page header, leaving little room before the primary CTA — on older devices it risked falling out of view entirely.</li>
		</ul>
	</div>
	<div>
		<h4>From business / platform</h4>
		<ul>
			<li>The business was wary of the new form generating more calls than the team could handle, without any way to prioritize which leads to call first.</li>
			<li>Most Funda visitors were still in their orientation phase, not ready to make an appointment on the spot.</li>
		</ul>
	</div>
</KeyFindings>

<EndProductSection
	heading="The End Product"
	intro="Six decisions carry the calculator: what users type in, what they get back, and how they move on with ING from inside someone else's page."
>
	<DecisionPair first>
		<Decision id="decision-a" badge="A" eyebrow="Ask only what the calculation needs" title="Changed all input fields" first>
			<p>Users struggled with De Hypotheker's input set: several fields weren't needed for the calculation, while a field to enter own money or surplus house value was missing entirely. To fix this, we took out the unneeded fields and split the wanted mortgage input ("gewenste hypotheekbedrag") into two: fictive bid and own money.</p>
			<ImageBlock src={oldVsNew} alt="Old single mortgage-amount field next to the new split fictief bod and eigen inleg fields" />
		</Decision>

		<Decision id="decision-b" badge="B" eyebrow="Answer the question behind the question" title="Add missing information" first>
			<p>Users wanted to know "kosten koper" — the additional costs that come with buying a house (notary costs, for example) — to judge whether their savings would cover the purchase. Adding this let users see if they had enough savings, or how much was left to put into the house.</p>
			<ImageBlock src={kostenKoper} alt="Close-up of kosten koper in the result card" variant="hatch" />
		</Decision>
	</DecisionPair>

	<Decision id="decision-c" badge="C" eyebrow="Keep the CTA in view" title="Branding in calculator">
		<p><strong>Technical constraint:</strong> mobile users arrived after a large page header, leaving little room before the primary CTA — on older devices it risked falling out of view entirely. I designed the logo into the result section to keep the CTA visible without adding height. On bigger devices, where space wasn't as tight, I added the option to show a brand image next to the result — I suggested a photo that looks back toward the result, to circle eye movement back to the follow-up actions.</p>
		<BeforeAfter
			src={brandingInCalc}
			alt="Logo tucked into the result card on mobile, brand photo beside the result on desktop"
			labels={['Mobile', 'Desktop']}
		/>
	</Decision>

	<DecisionPair>
		<Decision id="decision-d" badge="D" eyebrow="No dialogs allowed" title="Solving iframe restrictions" first>
			<p><strong>Technical constraint:</strong> because the calculator was framed into the Funda page, it wasn't possible to make use of dialogs. To work around that, I solved it inline by replacing the content of the card and adding back navigation — making it feel like a side-step navigation, so users don't lose direction.</p>
			<ImageBlock src={iframeNav} alt="Disclaimer and contact form states replacing the base card, each with back navigation" />
		</Decision>

		<Decision id="decision-e" badge="E" eyebrow="Lead quality vs. user friction" title="Contact form" first>
			<p><strong>Business constraint:</strong> the business was wary of the new form generating more calls than the team could handle, so (too) much qualification logic was requested. Together with the core team, I kept the logic to a minimum, and we agreed on: sending ING customers to the logged-in environment, where they could plan it in themselves, and letting non-ING customers fill in a form and be called back at a moment an ING employee had time, with a single urgency question to sort incoming requests.</p>
			<ImageBlock src={interviewForm} alt="ING-customer self-plan path next to the non-ING callback form with its urgency question" variant="hatch" maxWidth="536px" />
			<Note>Update: On Funda you will now find an even more simplified contact form. I wasn't involved in that part.</Note>
		</Decision>
	</DecisionPair>

	<Decision id="decision-f" badge="F" eyebrow="Orientation, not conversion" title="Down the rabbit hole">
		<p>The 0-measurement showed most Funda visitors were still in their orientation phase, not ready to make an appointment on the spot. Rather than pushing for immediate conversion, we kept them moving down the funnel with ING: giving them the option to calculate if they could actually afford the house they spotted and did a monthly cost calculation for.</p>
		<p>For this, we decided to send them to the ING website. This has benefits for usability and business purposes, because we have full control (instead of being framed into a website). On that website, they'd see a new calculator that reuses the input they already filled in on Funda, and they'd only need to answer their income(s). After that, we tried to let them continue with other mortgage orientation tools or making an appointment.</p>
		<ImageBlock src={handoffVisual} alt="The handoff: Funda calculator to prefilled ING calculator, income only, then the outcome" variant="hatch" />
	</Decision>
