			<div>
				<h4><em>Action-based risk rules</em></h4>
				<p>Participants had no issue with the new interaction, but questioned whether action-based rules could still achieve the same fraud coverage as before, since the simplified approach, while easier to use, is also inherently more limited.</p>
			</div>
			<div>
				<h4><em>AI configuration</em></h4>
				<p>In the old setup, a payment was blocked when the total of score-based rules triggered exceeded 100. This was made worse by users simultaneously having to adjust to action-based risk rules.</p>
			</div>
			<div>

	<DecisionToc items={[

		{ number: '02', title: 'AI as just another risk rule', href: '#decision-2' },
		{ number: '03', title: 'Introduce sorting by impact', href: '#decision-3' },
		{ number: '04', title: 'Introduce four blocking thresholds', href: '#decision-4' },
		{ number: '05', title: 'Show expected impact', href: '#decision-5' },

	<Decision id="decision-1" badge="01" eyebrow="Action-based risk rules" title="Re-use the tabs to represent the new hierarchy of actions" first>
		<p>The risk result is decided by the highest priority rule that triggers — Allow rules have a higher priority than Block, followed by Review, etc. Reusing the existing tab structure let us introduce the new action hierarchy without asking merchants to relearn a new layout, while also minimizing implementation effort for development. Validated usability during concept testing.</p>
		<BeforeAfter
			before={{ src: reuseTabsBefore, alt: 'Score-based profile with its long custom rule set', caption: 'Average enterprise account 100+ rules' }}
			after={{ src: reuseTabsAfter, alt: 'Action-based profile with Allow, Block, Review and Check for 3DS tabs', caption: '~20 rules' }}
		/>
		<BeforeAfter
			before={{ src: riskResultBefore, alt: 'Score-based risk results page with total risk score and triggered rules', caption: 'Score math' }}
			after={{ src: riskResultAfter, alt: 'Action-based risk result showing the blocking rule and its reasoning', caption: 'Blocked because of X rule' }}
		/>
	</Decision>

	<Decision id="decision-2" badge="02" eyebrow="Interaction with and integration of AI" title="AI as just another risk rule">
		<p>Research showed there would be a small learning curve, but with many advantages. We decided to move forward with AI as just another risk rule, since it could reuse existing interaction patterns, minimizing cognitive load as well as development effort now and in the future (e.g. self-serve analytics could reuse the same patterns).</p>
	</Decision>

	<Decision id="decision-3" badge="03" eyebrow="Interaction with and integration of AI" title="Introduce sorting by impact">
		<p>Sorting by impact highlights the outsized effect AI has, visually placing it at the top of the list. This also created a natural path toward a deeper dive into further analytics.</p>
		<ImageBlock src={sortingByImpact} alt="Impact-sorted rule list with AI rules at the top" variant="hatch" />
	</Decision>

	<Decision id="decision-4" badge="04" eyebrow="AI configuration" title="Introduce four blocking thresholds">
		<div>
			<p>There are five possible AI evaluation levels, but only four can be used as blocking thresholds — a very low option is technically possible, but would block all traffic.</p>
			<ul>
				<li>No scores, to avoid confusion with the mental model of the old score-based rules (learning from concept testing)</li>
				<li>Limited choices, to learn if more are needed</li>
				<li>Abstract labeling to keep focus on impact, while usable to share AI evaluation after the payment</li>
			</ul>
		</div>
		<ImageBlock src={aiConfiguration} alt="Four-threshold selector with backtest chart" variant="hatch" maxWidth="640px" />
	</Decision>

	<Decision id="decision-5" badge="05" eyebrow="AI configuration" title="Show expected impact">
		<p>Users can backtest the AI thresholds on a chosen merchant, starting with block rate and eventually expanding to all relevant metrics. This was essential, since Adyen can't share its "secret sauce" or explain it in a lightweight way.</p>
		<ImageBlock src={protectHero} alt="Final, polished screens of the rule-management interface" variant="hatch-bleed" />
	</Decision>

	<Decision id="decision-6" badge="06" eyebrow="AI evaluation explanation — a follow-up initiative I scoped and led" title="Redesign the payment-level AI evaluation explanation">
		<p>Testing surfaced a second risk beyond the interaction redesign itself: merchants didn't just need to operate the new AI controls, they needed to trust the AI's own output. Just a couple of wrongfully blocked transactions without explanation was enough to make them want to move traffic away. I scoped this into a follow-up initiative to redesign how that evaluation is explained at the payment level.</p>
		<Columns min="260px">
			<Quote attribution="Payment Manager X">"I got customer complaints that good users were being blocked by the AI — that's when I took a step back."</Quote>
			<Quote attribution="E-commerce Manager Y">"At the end of the day, when somebody asks me why, I need to have an answer — if machine learning does something, there needs to be an output of why."</Quote>
		</Columns>
		<BeforeAfter
			before={{ src: aiEvalBefore, alt: 'Fraud signal analysis screen listing raw signals without explanation', caption: 'No explanation' }}
			after={{ src: aiEvalAfter, alt: 'Machine learning fraud risk level signals grouped by strength', caption: 'Reasoning surfaced' }}
		/>
		<Callout tone="surface" heading="Why this had to ship before scale, not after">
			<Columns min="280px" gap="clamp(24px, 3.4vw, 40px)">
				<p>Testing and pilot data flagged that unexplained blocks would become a major blocker at scale — so this redesign shipped ahead of that exposure, not in response to an active support crisis.</p>
				<Stack gap="14px">
					<p>It unlocked two things for Payment Experts: spot-checking allowed and blocked transactions to fine-tune thresholds or add custom rules for their own business logic, and explaining blocked payments to customers directly, without looping in the risk team. The next problem it surfaced — feeding that feedback back into Protect automatically — was scoped for later.</p>
					<p>The impact shows up in the rollout numbers below.</p>
				</Stack>
			</Columns>
		</Callout>
	</Decision>
