	title="Journey Demo"
	roleTag="Senior UX Designer, Presenter"
	problem="ING's mortgage products existed as separate tools, but customers move through one continuous journey (Scope → Purchase). Upper management had little visibility into how those products connected, or a reason to prioritize joining them up."
	whatIDid="Built an interactive prototype walking through the full journey (Scope, Awareness, Orientation, Desire, Advice, Purchase), showing concrete sub-flows like a Ghost account (nudging Enzo to validate his calculations with an advisor) and the Bid calculator (turning a walk through his dream neighbourhood into a monthly-cost check that led him to want advisor confirmation)."
	whyItMattered="Presenting this to upper management made the fragmentation visible and gave them a shared reference for the journey — it became a tool for aligning stakeholders on cross-product strategy, not just a prototype demo."

<SnapshotProject
	id="result-cards"
	number="02"
	title="Calculator Result Cards"
	roleTag="Senior UX Designer"
	problem="Each mortgage calculator had its own result card. Once users had run two or three in one session, they'd end up looking at several separate cards and losing track of which numbers still applied."
	whatIDid="Designed one reusable result card that accumulates results as users complete each calculator — always showing the current combined picture instead of a pile of past ones."
	whyItMattered="Cut confusion at the exact moment users needed confidence to book an advisor appointment — and the pattern got reused across other mortgage processes, so this one decision paid off multiple times over."
>
	<ImageBlock src={resultCard} alt="Several standalone result cards next to the single accumulating card" />
</SnapshotProject>

<SnapshotProject
	id="max-widget"
	number="03"
	title="Max Mortgage Widget"
	roleTag="Senior UX Designer"
	problem={`The "Hypotheek berekenen" widget gave users a quick max-mortgage result, but the appointment CTA wasn't clearly converting as well as it could.`}
	whatIDid="Designed and prepared A/B experiments to test it: on mobile, a stripped-back variant (removed title inputs, header, and explanation text, with a higher-contrast background) to get the appointment CTA above the fold; on desktop, a parallel test of the same above-the-fold hypothesis. I also proposed a bigger structural change — consistent with how the widget appeared elsewhere in the flow — as the preferred long-term direction, flagging it as harder to align stakeholders on."
	whyItMattered='Moved the widget from "improve based on opinion" to "improve based on tested hypotheses," while being upfront about which change was quick to ship versus which needed more buy-in.'
