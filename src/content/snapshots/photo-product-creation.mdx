<SnapshotProject
	id="online-editor"
	number="01"
	title="Online Editor Ready for Photobooks"
	roleTag="Senior UX Designer, UX Researcher"
	problem="The Online (HTML5) editor was built first for simpler Albelli products (calendars, mugs, etc.), which needed far fewer editing options. That setup didn't scale 1-on-1 to photobooks, which needed much richer editing — so photobook customers were stuck with an editor designed for a simpler job."
	whatIDid="Redesigned the editor architecture and validated it with customers across all product types, so the same foundation could support both simple products and photobooks' greater complexity without forking the codebase."
	whyItMattered="Gave Albelli one editor platform instead of divergent ones per product line — photobook customers got the editing depth they needed, while calendar and mug customers kept the simplicity that suited them."

<SnapshotProject
	id="smart-assistant"
	number="02"
	title="Integration of Smart Assistant"
	roleTag="Senior UX Designer, UX Researcher"
	problem="Albelli had bought an AI engine (Smart Assistant) that could select the best photos from a big batch and auto-build a photobook — but it needed to slot into two existing, structurally different editors (Windows and Mac) without feeling bolted on."
	whatIDid="Designed how the AI hand-off worked in both editors, cutting the customer's input down to a small number of meaningful choices (like layout style) rather than exposing the AI's full complexity."
	whyItMattered="Turned a technically impressive but abstract capability into something a non-technical customer could trust in a few clicks — the layout-choice pattern became the template for how AI decisions got surfaced elsewhere in the editor."
>
	<Columns min="280px" gap="clamp(24px, 3.4vw, 40px)" align="start">

		<ImageBlock src={smartAssistant} alt="The choose-options step of the Smart Assistant with three layout style cards" />
	</Columns>
</SnapshotProject>
